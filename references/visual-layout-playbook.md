# Visual And Layout Playbook For Biomedical Reviews

Use this reference when planning or auditing the information hierarchy, figure/table system, page layout, or exported manuscript. It presents adaptable organizational patterns and visual checks. Typography, submission formatting, and permissions must follow the target journal or the user-provided template.

## 1. Transferable Architecture

### Center the review on one coupling or design problem

Define one sentence that explains what the literature collectively helps resolve. Test every major section, figure, and conclusion against it. A section that cannot connect to the sentence probably needs a stronger transition, a different role, or removal.

An abstract should communicate, in a compact sequence:

`object and scope -> organizing mechanism -> application relevance -> central bottlenecks -> translation route -> future direction -> unifying contribution`

### Build a reasoning chain rather than a material catalogue

A useful default for materials and bioengineering reviews is:

`fundamentals -> materials and interfaces -> synergies and tradeoffs -> fabrication and evaluation -> application evidence -> lab-to-clinic/lab-to-life translation -> challenges and research roadmap`

This is a decision aid, not a mandatory table of contents. Collapse, reorder, or omit stages when the review question requires a different center of gravity.

### Close with contribution, bottleneck, and route

Do not use the conclusion merely to repeat the abstract. State:

1. the framework or judgment added by the review;
2. the unresolved, testable bottlenecks;
3. a corresponding research route, evaluation standard, or translational milestone for each bottleneck.

## 2. Figure-Role System

Assign each figure one dominant cognitive job before designing it.

| Role | Reader question answered | Useful form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overview | What is the whole review about? | Concept map or system overview |
| Evolution | How did the field reach its current state? | Selective timeline with turning points |
| Taxonomy | What are the meaningful classes? | Hierarchy, matrix, or decision tree |
| Mechanism | How does the effect arise? | Multiscale mechanism diagram |
| Tradeoff | Which gains conflict, and where is the design window? | Tension map, Pareto view, or design space |
| Evidence | How is performance demonstrated and compared? | Evidence matrix or measurement chain |
| Application | Where does the platform create value? | Application map with shared comparison logic |
| Translation | What blocks movement beyond the lab? | Pipeline with gates and failure modes |
| Future agenda | Which challenge maps to which research direction? | Challenge-to-route roadmap |

Before adding a figure, compare its job with every existing figure. Merge or remove redundant roles. A large number of figures is not itself evidence of synthesis.

### Integrate each figure with prose

Use a three-part rhythm:

1. Before the figure, state why the reader needs the framework and what question it answers.
2. After the figure, explain its nodes or axes in the same order used visually.
3. End with a comparative inference, design rule, or transition that the image enables.

Do not leave a figure as an isolated illustration or retell every label verbatim.

### Control montage density

Use representative panels to prove a pattern, not to display every available example. If a composite approaches roughly 8-10 independently labeled panels, test whether two figures, a matrix, or supplementary material would produce a clearer argument. Judge the result at expected publication size, not only while zoomed in.

## 3. Section And Application Templates

### Section rhythm

For a substantial section, prefer:

`scope/reason -> classification or mechanism framework -> representative evidence -> horizontal comparison -> failure conditions and limitations -> synthesis/transition`

The order can vary, but all six functions should be discoverable. A sequence of study summaries without horizontal comparison is incomplete.

### Application comparison spine

Use the same questions across application subsections:

- What clinical or use scenario creates the need?
- Which function or endpoint matters?
- What is the dominant failure risk?
- Which material or device design addresses it?
- Which quantitative evidence and comparator support the claim?
- What safety, durability, workflow, or scale-up gap remains?

For biomedical reviews, record at minimum: sample/site, cell/animal/human model, control or baseline, endpoint and units, observation duration, safety/biocompatibility evidence, maturity level, and principal limitation.

### Tradeoff and failure-mode audit

For each major advantage, ask:

- What is sacrificed to obtain it?
- Under which environment, duration, loading, or biological condition does it fail?
- Is the claim based on mechanism, bench measurement, animal evidence, or human evidence?
- Which threshold would make it clinically or operationally meaningful?

Visualize the important conflicts when they govern the field: mechanics versus healing, conductivity versus stretchability, adhesion versus removability, sensitivity versus selectivity, performance versus breathability, durability versus degradation, or function versus manufacturability.

## 4. Tables As Comparison Infrastructure

Use tables for fields that readers must compare repeatedly; keep prose for interpretation and causal reasoning.

A biomedical materials evidence table can include:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Material/system and structure scale | Identifies the compared design |
| Fabrication or integration route | Reveals process constraints |
| Dominant mechanism | Links structure to function |
| Sample, model, and comparator | Anchors evidence context |
| Quantitative endpoint | Enables real comparison |
| Duration and durability | Prevents snapshot-only claims |
| Biocompatibility or safety | Distinguishes functional from translational evidence |
| Evidence maturity | Separates concept, in vitro, animal, human, and clinical levels |
| Main limitation | Prevents promotional selection |
| Source | Maintains traceability |

### Pagination rules

- Repeat column headings on every continuation page.
- Keep one record together when possible; do not split a logical row across pages without clear continuation markers.
- If cells become narrow stacks of words, shorten fields, use landscape orientation if allowed, divide one overloaded table by analytical purpose, or move exhaustive data to supplementary material.
- Define abbreviations in each table or its note; do not depend on distant prose.

## 5. Layout And Export QA

Inspect the rendered PDF, not only the editable source.

### Hierarchy and navigation

- Heading levels are visually distinct and used consistently.
- Long manuscripts have appropriate navigation, such as a contents page, bookmarks, or strong cross-references when the delivery format permits.
- Figure and table placement supports the reading sequence.
- Page breaks do not strand headings, captions, or transition sentences.

### Figures and tables

- Labels, legends, and annotations remain legible at expected publication size.
- Color is not the only carrier of meaning; contrast remains adequate when printed or viewed by readers with color-vision differences.
- Figure/table number, caption, in-text callout, and exported object all agree.
- Every cited figure/table exists; every included figure/table is cited.
- Continuation tables repeat their headers and preserve record integrity.
- Permissions or attribution text follows the target journal's rules without overwhelming the scientific message.

### Export integrity

- No unintended blank, duplicate, rotated, or missing pages.
- No clipped text, hidden layers, black boxes, broken glyphs, or rasterized text that should remain readable.
- Images are present in the body rather than only as detached submission attachments.
- Page numbering and headers/footers are intentional and consistent.
- References are readable and citation numbering remains synchronized after layout changes.

## 6. What Not To Generalize

- Do not copy peer-review wrappers such as submission metadata, continuous line numbering, manuscript page labels, or review-system headers.
- Do not infer ideal fonts, point sizes, margins, column count, reference style, or copyright wording from a single example manuscript. Obtain those from the target journal guide or the user's template.
- Do not treat a single-column review draft as a superior publication layout; it is optimized for review and annotation.
- Do not reproduce crowded multi-panel figures merely because they contain many examples. Preserve thematic grouping while reducing cognitive load.
- Do not imitate promotional wording. Match every strength claim to the evidence level and use cautious language for proposed or unvalidated benefits.
- Do not treat missing bookmarks, contents navigation, broken tables, or empty attachment pages as acceptable merely because they appear in an example document.
